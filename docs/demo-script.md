## Narration

Trawd is for self-employed tradespeople who read customer enquiries between jobs. A message can tell you something is wrong without saying what needs fixing, where it is, or when the customer wants help. Trawd turns that first message into a short brief a tradesperson can check before calling back.

I’ll start as the customer and type, “Something is broken in my kitchen.” Trawd asks for a photo because the problem is vague. A photo is optional and only for the tradesperson to inspect. I’ll skip it. The form asks five quick questions about the work, the area, the preferred time, the urgency, and any safety concern.

I’ll select repair, say that a cupboard door hinge is loose, and enter Cork and Wednesday afternoon. I’ll also report that a socket sometimes sparks. If I add that the problem started today, the form keeps Wednesday afternoon as my preferred visit time. Those are two different pieces of information.

When I submit, the tradesperson sees a separate brief. It keeps the hinge, Cork, Wednesday afternoon and the reported electrical concern. It suggests maintenance and an electrician, keeps the caution visible, and asks for qualified assessment. The original customer paragraph stays out of this view. The tradesperson still decides what to do.

One classmate found that changing Tuesday to Wednesday made the old prototype fall back to a generic response. I’m replaying that same edit on a clear plumbing enquiry. The revised brief keeps Wednesday, the location, and the practical follow-ups. The test report records both outputs so the change can be checked.

The proposed AI role is to organise messy text and identify missing information. This build uses prepared examples and limited rules. It has no live language model or image analysis, and the handoff stays in this browser tab. My next priority is to test unfamiliar fictional enquiries with tradespeople and see which details they still have to ask for.

## Recording a version in your own voice on Windows

1. Open the prototype in a clean browser tab and close unrelated tabs and notifications.
2. In Clipchamp, start a video and choose **Record & create**, then **Screen**. Allow microphone access and choose the microphone you intend to use.
3. Begin recording and select just the Trawd browser tab. Record ten seconds, stop and play back to check that the page is readable and your voice is clear.
4. Record the walkthrough above. Use **Stop sharing** to finish, save the capture to the project and check the final running time and sound in the preview.
5. Export at 1080p as `Trawd_Project_Prototype_Revision_Demo.mp4`.
